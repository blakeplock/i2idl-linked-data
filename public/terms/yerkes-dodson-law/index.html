Learning Sciences / Concept

Yerkes-Dodson law

Definition

A principle describing how performance can vary with arousal or stress, often represented as an inverted-U relationship whose optimal level depends on task characteristics.

Editorial explanation

It is a reminder that too little or too much stress can affect performance and that difficulty should be designed with context in mind.
